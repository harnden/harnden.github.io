The playbook — phases & steps
Phase 0 · Setup (first session only)
  1. Assign owners. One teammate each for Content Powerhouse, Communication Goals, Media Mix, and Schedule+Forecast — one person per decision page at a time; only disconnect inactive teammates.
  2. Pick the team name together. Max 8 characters; letters, digits, space, dash only.
  3. Confirm the budget in the home bar. Year 1: $12.5M — Urban 4.0 / Rural 4.8 / Tourism 3.7.
Phase 1 · Analyze
  1. Split the 8 ANALYZE reports. Confirm the Year 0 picture: MOJO #1 (29%), leads Millennials (40.2%), #2 in Gen Z (45.3 vs ROCX 50.2), locked out of Gen X (11.6%), best efficiency (63%), lowest awareness (69%).
  2. Check ROCX first in every competitor view — same price, same segments, the only real rival.
  3. Read each region's funnel rings on the Communication Goals screen: fat outer / thin inner → Action; thin outer ring → Awareness.
Phase 2 · Decide
  1. Content Powerhouse: 10–20% of budget (~$1.3–2.5M) across Social Listening / Data Analytics / Creativity, each split ~45/45/10 toward Millennials/Gen Z; use the helper charts; reach max insight quality in 1–2 years, then taper.
  2. Communication Goals per region: default Action first (60%), Awareness second (40%) — override only where step 6 showed a different funnel break.
  3. Media Mix per region: Urban digital-first (social, mobile, SEO; TV for Gen Z's traditional slice) · Rural adds real print/radio/billboards (Gen X 85% offline; Millennials read print there) · Tourism heaviest digital plus a modest sponsored-events bet timed to Tennis GS. Favor high-ROMI types everywhere (Social 8.16 · TV 6.90 · SEO 6.60 · Mobile 6.46); starve viral, blogs, display. Minimal Gen X spend outside Rural.
  4. Media Schedule per region: Tourism = Month, front-loaded May–Aug (Tennis GS + tourist peak) · Rural = Quarter, weight Q2 & Q4 · Urban = Year/Semester.
  5. Forecast conservatively. Assume ROCX contests every gain; verify projected EBT stays healthy in all three regions (budget ≈ 20% of regional EBT — every $1M of EBT ≈ $200K next year).
Phase 3 · Run
  1. Fix all errors; read every warning — warnings catch things like a media category left at zero.
  2. Re-check expenses vs. authorized budget in the home bar.
  3. Full-team huddle, then RUN. Irreversible; five total.
Phase 4 · After results
  1. Diagnose before deciding: actual vs. forecast, ROCX's moves, new funnel breaks, fresh ROMI numbers — returns shift as the mix changes.
  2. Re-pull events & seasonality for the new year. Tennis GS was next year only; events change annually.

Key rules to remember

  • Budget compounds. Next year's budget is set by this year's EBT per region:
    EBT ≤ $30MBudget = $6M
    $30M ≤ EBT ≤ $60MBudget = 20% × EBT
    EBT ≥ $60MBudget = $9M + 5% × EBT
  • Two scorecards. Purchase Funnel Performance (PFP) measures media management; the Overall Performance Index (OPI) combines sales, share, and profit — and matters most.
  • Errors block the run (e.g., overspending). Warnings deserve a careful look before running.
  • One person per decision page. Divide areas among teammates; only disconnect inactive teammates.
  • Get team sign-off before RUN. It cannot be cancelled or rolled back.

Year 0 data readout — the battle map

Millennials — defend the lead

  • MOJO #1 at 40.2% share (ROCX 36.3% close behind)
  • Funnel break: preference 67.1% → intention 36.6% → Action objective
  • Segment overtakes Gen X in size by year 5 (12,494 KU)
  • Media: 70% digital, balanced owned mix, SEO strongest in paid, viral strongest in earned

Gen Z — winnable knife-fight

  • MOJO #2 at 45.3% vs ROCX 50.2% — a 5-point gap
  • Funnel break: awareness 66% is lowest of all four brands → Awareness objective
  • ~50% segment growth over five years
  • Media: mobile apps 50% + social 40% of owned; SEO 50% of paid; blogs/viral in earned; traditional slice = TV 80%

Gen X — expensive uphill war

  • MOJO only 11.6% — TOIZ 42.9% and SOFT 39.6% are entrenched
  • Preference 47% but intention 13.5% — a costly gap to fix against strong incumbents
  • Largest segment today, but lowest growth; don't overinvest early
  • If contested: print 40% > TV 30% in traditional; sponsored events, website, reviews dominate their other categories

Media mix by region — habits differ, don't copy-paste

  • Urban: digital-first for Gen Z/Millennials (Owned 40/30, mobile apps & social lead); TV is 80% of Gen Z's traditional slice
  • Rural: notably traditional — Gen X 85% offline (print 50%), Millennials' print/radio at 40/40 and display ads tie SEO; add real print, radio & billboard weight here
  • Tourism: most digital — Gen Z Owned 45% (social/mobile 40/40), paid = SEO/PPC 45/45; sponsored events are 50% of Millennials' outdoor — pair with Tennis GS in May–Jun
  • Blend each region's mix by its dominant segments, then overweight the high-ROMI types within categories

Competitive position — ROCX is the arch-rival

  • MOJO is already #1: 29.0% unit share vs ROCX 26.3%, SOFT 23.5%, TOIZ 21.3%
  • ROCX mirrors you: $14.10 price, $5.70 cost, same value segments — and leads you in Gen Z
  • Dollar race is tighter (26.7% vs SOFT 25.0%) — premium brands monetize better per can
  • Check ROCX's moves first in every competitor report

Price & distribution facts

  • Value tier: MOJO $14 / ROCX $14.10 · Premium tier: SOFT $16.20 / TOIZ $17
  • Premium pricing is why SOFT+TOIZ own 82% of Gen X — and price is a management call, not a media one; Gen X is structurally out of reach
  • Low price is a tailwind with price-sensitive Gen Z and bargain-hunting Millennials
  • Distribution 41% vs TOIZ's 45% — the Action objective raises coverage, unlocking stuck purchase intention

Measured ROMI per $ spent — reallocate accordingly

  • Winners: Social Media 8.16 · TV 6.90 · SEO 6.60 · Mobile Apps 6.46 · Print 5.87 · Web 5.67
  • Losers: Sponsored Events 0.12 · Viral 0.45 · Blogs 2.80 · Display Ads 3.23 · PPC 3.41
  • Overrides the handbook's sponsorship suggestion — trust measured returns
  • Caveat: ROMI reflects the current mix — and events scored 0.12 in a year with no events; Tennis GS + Tourism's event-heavy habits justify a small, measured bet there

Budget & efficiency facts

  • Year 1 budget: $12.5M — Urban 4.0 / Rural 4.8 / Tourism 3.7
  • Empirically, budget = 20% of regional EBT — every $1M of EBT earns $200K of next year's budget
  • MOJO's comms efficiency 63% — best in market (ROCX 55, SOFT 48, TOIZ 32); lowest awareness (69%) yet highest share — awareness is the weak rung
  • All four firms have near-identical EBT and budgets — win on allocation, not outspending
  • Seasonality and events differ sharply by region — see the regional rhythms card

Regional rhythms — schedule each region differently

  • Tourism: Tennis GS event May–Jun (~1.3M extra visitors) + strong Aug peak (~25% swing) → Month timing, front-load May–Aug. ROCX outsells MOJO here every month — the knife-fight region.
  • Rural: peaks May & Nov, trough in Aug — the inverse of Tourism → Quarter timing, weight Q2 & Q4. MOJO's fortress: biggest lead, biggest EBT ($24.1M), biggest budget ($4.8M).
  • Urban: flat with a mild Sep–Nov bump, no events → Year or Semester timing is fine.

Insights & implications

Growth lives with Millennials & Gen Z — and so does MOJO's position

Stated Handbook: Gen X is the largest segment. Year 0 data: MOJO holds just 11.6% of it, vs 40.2% of Millennials (#1) and 45.3% of Gen Z (#2).

Implication The "milk Gen X" play doesn't exist for MOJO — the incumbents own it. Fight where you're strong and where growth is: defend Millennials, close the 5-point Gen Z gap.

Balanced regional profit beats one dominant region

Stated Budget is computed per region, then summed. Marginal return is 20% of EBT between $30–60M but only 5% above $60M.

Implication Three regions at $60M EBT each → ~$36M budget. One region at $180M + two weak → ~$30M. Don't starve two regions to feed one.

Pick the first objective off the funnel data — it carries 60%

Stated Awareness → recognition · Interest → consideration/preference/intention · Action → distribution & share · Engagement → loyalty & advocacy.

Implication Diagnose where each region's funnel breaks. If awareness is 80% but purchase intention is 38%, more Awareness spend is wasted — target the bottleneck.

Match objectives to segment psychology

Stated Millennials are less brand loyal and bargain-hunt. Gen X prefers premium; price signals quality. Gen Z is ad-skeptical.

Implication Action (promos, availability) fits Millennials; Engagement fights their nature. Heavy promotion may cheapen the brand for Gen X — Interest/Engagement fit better. Earned media & advocacy punch above their weight with Gen Z.

Gen Z requires multi-channel — don't go all-digital

Stated Short attention spans; best reached via multiple channels, e.g. sponsorship and print to complement a social or mobile campaign.

Implication Dumping 100% into social/mobile for the "digital natives" is the exact mistake this line warns against.

Media mapping is mostly handed to you

Stated Gen X: TV first, print second, plus internet/email. Millennials: mobile and social throughout the day, every day.

Implication Open with allocations that follow these habits; fine-tune with in-game media consumption reports.

Seasonality is a real lever — especially Tourism

Stated The doc's own example bumps Jul/Aug spend in Tourism and Q2 in Urban, and says to review next year's Events to reallocate spending and gain sales.

Implication Teams that leave timing on "Year / even" all five rounds leave sales on the table. Graduate to quarter/month timing as you learn the patterns.

Insight quality is a multiplier, not a cost

Stated High-quality insights increase the impact of messages; you can stop investing once max quality is reached.

Implication Underspending Content Powerhouse makes every downstream dollar work less hard. Treat it as a front-loaded investment, not a permanent tax.

Competitors react — forecast conservatively

Stated Competitors are dynamic and will react to what is happening in the market.

Implication Assume your share gains are contested when entering forecast estimates. Rosy forecasts lead to overbuilt plans and budget misses.
